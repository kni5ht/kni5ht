Abstract

LightEchoNet is a lightweight audio classification framework that satisfies the most stringent edge‑device constraints (<128 KB, <30 MMACs) while remaining competitive with models orders of magnitude larger. The student architecture combines depthwise separable convolutions, residual connections, residual normalization, and attentive global average pooling for efficient and robust feature extraction. To close the performance gap under extreme compression, we propose a two‑stage knowledge distillation strategy that transfers complementary knowledge from a heterogeneous teacher ensemble, including a CLAP‑based multimodal model with a lightweight audio encoder, thereby bringing semantic supervision to the tiny‑model regime. Intermediate feature matching with Huber loss and logit‑level distillation enables effective optimization.

LightEchoNet achieves 60.0% accuracy on TAU Urban Acoustic Scenes 2022 Mobile, 92.0% on ESC‑50, 58.7% mAP on FSD50K, and 97.4% on UrbanSound8K, using 350× fewer parameters than large‑scale pretrained models.

Motivation

Deploying audio classification models on mobile, wearable, and embedded devices is challenging because these platforms have strict limits on memory, computation, storage, and power. Although large pretrained models achieve strong performance, they are often too expensive for practical deployment. Compact models are more efficient, but reducing their size can weaken their ability to learn robust audio representations, especially under background noise, acoustic variability, and recording-device mismatch.

Motivational Points

  • Accuracy–efficiency trade-off: Lightweight models must remain accurate while using very few parameters and operations.
  • Limitations of conventional distillation: Output-level knowledge distillation does not fully transfer the teacher's intermediate representations.
  • Architectural mismatch: Direct feature matching is difficult when the teacher and student have different structures and feature dimensions.
  • No extra deployment cost: The student should benefit from powerful teachers during training without increasing its inference-time complexity.
  • Complementary supervision: Combining a structurally aligned inner teacher with a strong outer teacher can provide both feature-level and output-level guidance.
Architecture Details
Figure 1: Shared LE architecture for student and inner teacher.

Shared Student–Inner-Teacher Backbone

Figure 1 presents the structurally aligned Light Echo architectures used by the compact student and the inner teacher. Both networks follow the same stage organization and feature dimensions, containing seven LE Blocks arranged as 3+1+3 blocks and nine T–S Block positions in total. This shared structure allows their intermediate features to be compared directly without additional projection layers.

A T–S Block is a shared architectural position whose implementation depends on the network:

  • In the student, it is implemented as a standard 3×3 convolution.
  • In the inner teacher, it is replaced by the more expressive FusionBlock, also referred to as FusionConv. As detailed in Figure 2, this block processes features through parallel 3×3, 5×5, 7×7, and 3×1 convolutional branches, followed by Batch Normalization, concatenation, and a 1×1 fusion convolution.

Seven of the nine T–S Blocks appear inside the seven LE Blocks, while the remaining two are located in the convolutional stem. Each LE Block contains two branches. The transformation branch applies Batch Normalization, a 1×1 pointwise convolution, LeakyReLU, another Batch Normalization layer, a depthwise T–S operation, and a second LeakyReLU. The projection branch applies a pointwise convolution directly to the input, and the outputs of the two branches are added through a residual connection. ResNorm is used between the main stages, while AttnGAP, Batch Normalization, dropout, and a linear layer form the final classification head.

Figure 2: Staged distillation pipeline with inner and outer teachers.

Staged Inner–Outer Knowledge Distillation

Figure 2 illustrates the proposed staged distillation pipeline. First, the structurally aligned inner teacher transfers both intermediate representations and output-level knowledge to the student. Because the two networks have matching stages and feature dimensions, features from four approximately evenly spaced layers can be matched directly. The student is then refined using an outer teacher, which can be either a single high-performance model or an ensemble of teachers.

The framework therefore combines three forms of supervision:

  • Label supervision, using cross-entropy with the ground-truth labels.
  • Inner distillation, consisting of temperature-scaled logit distillation and normalized Huber-based feature matching.
  • Outer distillation, using temperature-scaled output probabilities from a single teacher or the average prediction of an ensemble.

Let \(z_s\), \(z_i\), and \(z_o\) denote the logits of the student, inner teacher, and a single outer teacher, respectively. The outer-teacher target is:

\[ p_{o}^{T} = \begin{cases} \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\dfrac{z_o}{T}\right), & \text{single model}, \\[8pt] \dfrac{1}{K}\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K} \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\dfrac{z_k}{T}\right), & \text{ensemble}. \end{cases} \]

The distillation distributions are defined as:

\[ p_{t}^{T} = \begin{cases} \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\dfrac{z_i}{T}\right), & \text{inner KD}, \\[8pt] p_o^T, & \text{outer KD}, \end{cases} \qquad p_s^T = \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(\dfrac{z_s}{T}\right). \]

For feature-level inner distillation, student and inner-teacher features at layer \(l\) are \(\ell_2\)-normalized:

\[ \widehat{F}_s^{(l)} = \frac{F_s^{(l)}} {\left\lVert F_s^{(l)} \right\rVert_2+\varepsilon}, \qquad \widehat{F}_i^{(l)} = \frac{F_i^{(l)}} {\left\lVert F_i^{(l)} \right\rVert_2+\varepsilon}. \]

The feature-matching loss over the aligned layer set \(S\) is:

\[ \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{feat}} = \frac{1}{|S|} \sum_{l\in S} \frac{1}{N_l} \sum_{j=1}^{N_l} \rho_{\delta} \left( \widehat{f}_{s,j}^{(l)} - \operatorname{sg} \left( \widehat{f}_{i,j}^{(l)} \right) \right), \]

where \(\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)\) stops gradients through the teacher and \(\delta = 1\). The Huber function is:

\[ \rho_{\delta}(u) = \begin{cases} \dfrac{1}{2}u^2, & |u|\leq\delta, \\[8pt] \delta\left(|u|-\dfrac{\delta}{2}\right), & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases} \]

The complete inner-distillation objective is:

\[ \mathcal{L} = (1-\alpha)\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}(z_s,y) + \alpha T^2\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{KL}} \left( p_t^T \,\Vert\, p_s^T \right) + \beta\, \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{feat}}. \]

Here, \(\alpha\) balances ground-truth and logit supervision, while \(\beta\) controls feature matching. During outer-teacher refinement, supervision is provided through cross-entropy and output-level KL distillation, without inner feature matching. After training, both teachers are discarded, so the final deployed model retains exactly the student's original architecture and inference cost.

Key Contributions

Efficient Student

  • Depthwise separable convolutions (8‑9× fewer FLOPs)
  • Residual connections + Residual Normalization after each stage
  • Attentive GAP (AttnGAP) for feature aggregation
  • Hybrid BN+ResNorm for cross‑device robustness

Two‑Stage KD

  • Inner teacher (CNN‑based) with Huber feature matching
  • Outer ensemble: CP‑Mobile + HTS‑AT + CLAP with lightweight audio encoder
  • Temperature‑scaled KL divergence for logit distillation
  • Stage‑wise hyperparameters: α=0.6→0.95, T=5.0→8.0
